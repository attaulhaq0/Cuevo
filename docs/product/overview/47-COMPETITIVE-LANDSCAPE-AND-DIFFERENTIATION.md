# Competitive Landscape and Differentiation

## Baseline competitors

### Toddle
Broad K–12 platform across curriculum, assessment, portfolios, reports, attendance/timetable, communication, behavior, accreditation and AI.

### ManageBac+
International/multi-curriculum school platform with curriculum, assessment, reporting, attendance, academic analytics and AI.

### Schoolbox
Whole-school learning platform with curriculum, learning, assessment, evidence, reporting, portfolios and integrations.

### iSAMS / Veracross
Strong school operating/SIS foundations with broad integrations and/or learning workflows.

## Strategic conclusion

Edeviser should achieve broad functional parity over time.

But breadth itself is not the moat.

## Edeviser differentiation hypothesis

```text
School context
+
Learning activity
+
Curriculum
+
Assessment
+
Evidence
+
Learner development
+
Intervention
+
Measured outcome
=
longitudinal learning evidence graph
```

Then:

```text
Evidence graph
 ↓
Edeviser Intelligence
 ↓
next action
 ↓
human approval
 ↓
intervention
 ↓
measurement
```

## Why this could be defensible

Potential assets:
- curriculum abstraction and verified packs
- evidence provenance
- longitudinal learner state
- intervention history
- measured outcomes
- school workflow integration
- institution policy/configuration
- accumulated evaluations and outcome data

Not defensible by itself:
- Next.js
- Supabase
- chatbot
- generic AI
- XP
- dashboards

## Competitive rule

Never pitch:
“we are Toddle but with AI.”

Pitch:
“we provide a connected learning-improvement system whose intelligence operates over governed longitudinal evidence.”

This remains a product hypothesis, not a guaranteed moat.
